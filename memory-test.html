What is a number memory test?

A number memory test measures your digit span — how many digits you can hold in short-term memory at once and repeat back correctly. The format above is the standard one: a number appears for a few seconds, disappears, and you type it from memory. Each round adds one more digit, so the test finds your ceiling rather than giving you a pass or fail.

It is one of the oldest measures in psychology and still appears as a subtest of the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale, where it is used to assess short-term and working memory.

What is a good score on a number memory test?

Because the typical span clusters tightly, the bands are narrow:

  • 5 or fewer — below the usual range. Very often this is distraction rather than memory; try again somewhere quiet.
  • 6 to 8 — where most adults land. This is the normal range, not a weak result.
  • 9 to 11 — above typical. Most people scoring here are chunking, deliberately or not.
  • 12 or more — unusual, and almost always a learned strategy rather than raw recall.

One caveat worth knowing: scores drift down when you are tired, rushed, or reading on a noisy screen. A single run is a rough estimate. Three runs on different days is a real number.

What is the average digit span?

About seven. The classic reference is George Miller's 1956 paper The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two, which put the span of immediate memory at roughly seven items for most adults — with normal variation of about two either side. That is where the familiar "seven digits" figure comes from, and it is also why phone numbers were originally seven digits long.

How do I improve my number memory?

Chunking is the single biggest lever. Nobody remembers 4 8 2 9 1 6 3 as seven separate digits — they remember 482, 916, 3. Grouping into twos or threes converts seven items into three, and three fits comfortably. Reading the digits in a rhythm, the way you say a phone number aloud, does the same job.

Beyond that, digit span responds to repetition: the more runs you do, the more automatic the grouping becomes. Gains show up fastest on the task you actually practise, so consistency matters more than session length — a few minutes daily beats an hour once a week.

Does digit span measure IQ?

Not on its own. Digit span is part of standardised IQ batteries, but it captures one narrow ability — short-term retention — rather than general intelligence. A high score means you hold sequences well. It does not predict reasoning, vocabulary, or problem-solving, and no single online test can give you an IQ.
